Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF)

Combines two ranked lists by summing reciprocal-rank contributions: score(d) = Σ 1/(k + rankD(d)) + Σ 1/(k + rankM(d)). Robust when raw scores from documents and memories are on different scales. The strongest fusion that needs no score calibration at all: mean R@10 of 51.32 across our three benchmarks, behind only Z-Agreement's 54.26.

Fused (RRF)

    score(d) = Σ 1/(k + rankD(d)) + Σ 1/(k + rankM(d))   (k=60)
    two rankings

    Reciprocal Rank Fusion ignores raw scores and uses positions. Documents in both lists get summed contributions and rise to the top.

    Parameters

    NameTypeDefaultDescription
    fusion"rrf"—Reciprocal Rank Fusion.
    rrf_kint60Smoothing constant — the standard from Cormack, Clarke, and Buettcher (2009).

    Why RRF over score-summing?

    Scores from the document view and the memory view live on different scales — the memory view tends to produce sharper distributions because verbalized triples match query phrasing more tightly. Summing raw scores would let one view dominate.

    RRF sidesteps the calibration question by working in rank space only. Top docs in both lists contribute large reciprocal-rank values; their sum surfaces them to the top of the fused ranking.

    Compared to agreement fusion

    On MuSiQue (in-distribution, well-calibrated scores), agreement fusion edges out RRF on R@10, and it also wins on the mean across all three benchmarks (54.26 vs 51.32 R@10). RRF stays the safer choice when the two views' scores cannot be assumed comparable, because it never looks at a score — only at ranks.
