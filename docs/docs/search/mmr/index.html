MMR

Maximal Marginal Relevance at every hop: pick the top-b candidates that maximize λ·sim(ct, x) − (1 − λ)·maxy∈retrieved sim(x, y). Lower λ spreads siblings across the semantic space — useful for width-oriented tasks (FanOutQA).

    max_hops=4,

When MMR helps — and when it doesn't

The optimal retrieval setup is strongly dataset-dependent. The single-modality winners in our results are not the same across benchmarks — Memory-Only Full Expansion is best on width-oriented FanOutQA (31.45 R@5) while Document-Only Full Tree is best on web-scale BrowseComp+ (21.18). MMR's fit follows the same axis.

  • Recommended: corpus="memory_only", search="mmr", lambda_mmr=0.5–0.7 on width-oriented tasks (FanOutQA-style) where many distinct facts contribute to the answer. Memory-only is already strong here; MMR's diversity bonus stacks because the encoder otherwise returns several near-paraphrases of the same memory.
  • Useful diagnostic: on any task where the top-k returned by Full Expansion contains near-duplicate items, swap to MMR with λ ≈ 0.5 and see whether Recall@10 improves while Recall@5 stays comparable.
  • Avoid on depth-oriented tasks (MuSiQue-style chains of reasoning). Sibling diversity is the wrong objective when you want the most relevant next fact along a known chain.
  • Avoid on small/noisy corpora. When relevant items are already sparse, the diversity penalty pushes the search away from them.

MMR composes with every corpus mode and every fusion method.
